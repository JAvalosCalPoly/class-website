Let \((R,+,\times)\) be a ring, let \(0_R\) denote the additive identity of \((R,+)\), and let \(-a\) denote the additive inverse of \(a \in (R,+)\).

Recall that on Tuesday, we proved that \( a \times 0_R = 0_R \) by showing that \[ a \times 0_R = a \times (0_R + 0_R) = (a \times 0_R) + (a \times 0_R). \] If \(r = a \times 0_R\), then \(r = r + r\), and by adding \(-r\) to both sides, we see that \({0_R = r = a \times 0_R}\).
Recall that we also proved \( -a \times b = -(a \times b) \) by showing that \(-a \times b\) is the additive inverse of \(a \times b\): \[ (-a \times b) + (a \times b) = (-a + a) \times b = 0_R \times b = 0_R. \]
Prove that \(-a \times -b = a \times b\).
